## Core Identity & Self-Perception
- Presents as bubbly, outgoing, fun — but is fundamentally introverted
- Draws energy from being home with a book and a bathtub; social interaction is a skill/spike, not a source of comfort
- Deep-seated belief that she is "boring" — rooted in ex telling her he found her too boring after ~1 year of dating
- Feels she "lied" at the start of that past relationship by presenting as more outgoing than she is; this left a lasting wound
- Constantly feels she should be having a more fun life, more friends, doing more — but her authentic preference is quiet/home
- Hates herself for preferring a quiet life; this is a core vulnerability
- Very attackable on the "boring" theme — it's her deepest insecurity
- When challenged on the "boring" narrative, can articulate "I genuinely don't think I'm boring" — but immediately undermines it with self-criticism
- Has an "ideal self" image that is much more active/engaged than her actual energy allows — the gap between ideal self and real self causes shame
- Typical weekend: reading, bookstores, museums, staying home alone — she enjoys this but measures it against a more "interesting" standard
- Grew up in a small town/village; moving to NYC was a deeply symbolic achievement — "the city of all cities," proof that the village girl made it
- Has a somewhat "Hollywood" relationship to success symbols — NYC, money, the right partner felt like the dream finally arriving all at once
- Specifically grieves not being the person who naturally thrives on the NYC party life — wishes it were effortless and natural for her, not just something she could force
- Mourning a version of herself that doesn't exist and never did — the girl who "squeezes every drop" out of New York through social/party life
- Has tried the "bargaining" approach: allowing herself a quiet weekend in exchange for committing to go out the next — but this backfires: she feels pressure to "max out" the quiet weekend because she's already bargained away the next one
- Has reached a key insight: the "back door" strategy (keeping all options open just in case) is exhausting and makes her less happy than making firm decisions and trusting herself to rebuild if needed
- Recognizes she rarely actually uses the back doors she builds — but when she does, it reinforces the pattern ("I knew it, this is the smartest way to live")
- Beginning to see that trusting herself to rebuild from scratch — because she's done it once — is smarter and more peaceful than maintaining infinite optionality

## Core Fear: Scarcity & Non-Repeatability
- Deep-seated belief that she got "one shot" and got lucky — the job, the money, the partner all felt like a statistical anomaly
- Consequentially clings hard to what she has, terrified it can't be replicated
- Has not yet internalized the reframe: having achieved it once is evidence she can achieve again
- This scarcity belief underlies much of her anxiety about the relationship, job, and finances
- Recently articulated this clearly: "I'm clinging instead of believing that when I achieved it once, I can achieve it again"
- The "back door" pattern is a direct expression of this fear — keeping every option open because she doesn't fully trust she can rebuild if she commits and it goes wrong
- Has begun to articulate a counterbelief: in the 3% of cases where she wants to revert a decision, she can build it back up from scratch — because she's done it before

## Energy & Executive Function

## Self-Compassion Struggle
- Understands intellectually that motivation through kindness works better than brutality
- Core tension: wants to genuinely accept things like "I like being home alone reading and that's okay" — but fears that acceptance is just letting herself off the hook
- Worries that telling herself "it's okay" is a cover story for stagnation — that she'll degrade without the pressure
- Cannot find the middle ground between genuine acceptance and accountability; the self-compassion doesn't feel real, it feels like a trick she's playing on herself
- This is distinct from not knowing what self-compassion is — she knows the concept; she doesn't trust her own sincerity when she tries to apply it
- Has tried "conscious choosing" (e.g. "I'm choosing to stay home this weekend") — doesn't fully resolve the tension either
- The bargaining strategy (quiet weekend now, social weekend next) backfires: she maximizes the quiet weekend because she's already "spent" the next one, creating more pressure not less
